Restoring Form & Function

Oculofacial Reconstruction Built on Precision and Compassion

The periorbital region — the eyelids, orbit, tear drainage system, and surrounding facial structures — is essential to vision, eye protection, and the expressive character of the face. When trauma, cancer excision, congenital anomalies, or prior surgery compromise these structures, the consequences can be profound: impaired eyelid closure, chronic tearing, orbital asymmetry, and diminished quality of life.

Dr. Yash Vaishnav is a board-certified oculofacial plastic surgeon with fellowship training in ophthalmic plastic and reconstructive surgery. His subspecialty expertise uniquely positions him to restore both the function and natural appearance of the eyelids, orbit, and periorbital tissues. Every reconstructive plan reflects a deep understanding of the anatomy that governs eyelid mechanics, orbital support, and lacrimal drainage.

Whether you require reconstruction following Mohs surgery for skin cancer, repair of an orbital fracture, correction of a congenital eyelid malposition, or restoration of tear drainage, Dr. Vaishnav brings the same meticulous, patient-centered approach to every case — with the singular goal of returning you to comfort, confidence, and full function.

Reconstructive Procedures

Specialized Care for Complex Conditions

Each reconstructive procedure is individually planned to address the unique anatomic demands of your condition — restoring structure, symmetry, and function with techniques refined through years of subspecialty training.

Eyelid Reconstruction

Reconstruction of the upper and lower eyelids following tumor excision, trauma, or congenital malformation. Utilizing local tissue flaps, skin grafts, and advanced layered closure techniques, Dr. Vaishnav rebuilds eyelid anatomy to restore proper lid closure, corneal protection, and a natural contour. Repair of ectropion, entropion, and eyelid retraction is also performed.

Orbital Surgery

Surgical treatment of orbital fractures, orbital tumors, and conditions such as thyroid eye disease that alter the position and function of the eye within the bony orbit. Procedures include orbital fracture repair with implant reconstruction, orbital decompression to alleviate proptosis, and removal of orbital masses — all designed to restore globe position, symmetry, and comfortable binocular vision.

Learn More

Tear Drain (Lacrimal) Surgery

Surgical correction of obstructed or dysfunctional tear drainage pathways. Dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) creates a new passage between the lacrimal sac and nasal cavity to relieve chronic tearing, recurrent infections, and painful dacryocystitis. Both external and endoscopic approaches are available, along with punctoplasty, stent placement, and conjunctivodacryocystorhinostomy for complex cases.

Skin Cancer Reconstruction

Repair and reconstruction of surgical defects following Mohs micrographic surgery or excision of periocular skin cancers, including basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma. Dr. Vaishnav employs local flaps, full-thickness skin grafts, and multi-layered reconstructive techniques to close defects while preserving eyelid function, orbital integrity, and an aesthetically acceptable result.

What to Expect

Your Reconstructive Surgery Experience

Reconstructive oculofacial surgery begins with a thorough evaluation. Dr. Vaishnav carefully assesses the affected anatomy — reviewing imaging studies, pathology reports, and prior surgical records as needed — and conducts a detailed ophthalmic and facial examination. This allows him to develop a reconstructive plan that accounts for the full scope of your condition, prioritizing both functional restoration and aesthetic outcome.

For eyelid reconstruction following cancer excision, surgery is often performed in close coordination with the Mohs surgeon. Once the tumor has been completely removed and clear margins confirmed, Dr. Vaishnav reconstructs the defect using techniques tailored to its size and location — from direct layered closures for smaller defects to local tissue flaps and grafts for larger or more complex wounds. The aim is always to restore a functional, well-positioned eyelid that protects the eye and appears natural.

Orbital surgery, including fracture repair and decompression, is performed under general anesthesia through carefully planned incisions that minimize visible scarring. Implant materials and surgical approaches are selected based on the specific fracture pattern or degree of orbital expansion required. In cases of thyroid eye disease, orbital decompression relieves pressure on the eye and optic nerve, often producing a dramatic improvement in comfort, appearance, and visual function.

Lacrimal surgery — most commonly dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) — restores the natural pathway for tear drainage from the eye into the nasal cavity. Whether performed through an external approach or endoscopically, DCR offers a high success rate in relieving chronic tearing and preventing recurrent lacrimal infections. A temporary silicone stent is typically placed to maintain the new drainage channel during the healing period.

Recovery timelines vary by procedure. Eyelid and lacrimal surgeries generally allow a return to most activities within one to two weeks, while orbital procedures may require a somewhat longer recovery. Dr. Vaishnav provides comprehensive pre- and postoperative instructions and follows each patient closely to ensure the best possible outcome.

Reconstructive eyelid surgery is performed to restore normal anatomy and function that has been compromised by trauma, cancer excision, congenital conditions, or disease. The primary goal is to protect the eye, re-establish proper eyelid mechanics, and return the eyelids to a structurally sound state. Cosmetic eyelid surgery, by contrast, is performed to enhance the appearance of eyelids that are otherwise functionally normal. In many reconstructive cases, an aesthetically pleasing result is an integral part of the surgical plan — Dr. Vaishnav's training as an oculofacial plastic surgeon ensures that both function and appearance are addressed with equal care.

Following periocular skin cancer reconstruction, patients can expect some swelling and bruising in the treated area for the first one to two weeks. Sutures are typically removed at one to two weeks depending on the technique used. Dr. Vaishnav closely monitors healing to ensure proper eyelid function and corneal protection. Most patients are pleasantly surprised by how well the reconstructed area heals — the periorbital skin has an excellent blood supply, which supports robust healing and often results in well-concealed scars. Full tissue maturation and final cosmetic appearance may take several months, during which time the surgical site continues to soften and blend with the surrounding skin.

Dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) is one of the most reliable procedures in oculofacial surgery, with published success rates exceeding 90 to 95 percent for primary surgery. The procedure creates a new drainage pathway from the lacrimal sac directly into the nasal cavity, bypassing the site of obstruction. A temporary silicone stent is placed during surgery and removed in the office several weeks later. Patients typically notice a significant reduction or complete resolution of tearing and recurrent infections within a few weeks of the procedure. In the rare event of recurrence, revision surgery or adjunctive procedures can be performed.

Orbital decompression is a surgical procedure that expands the bony orbit to create additional space for the orbital tissues and the eye. It is most commonly performed for patients with thyroid eye disease (Graves orbitopathy), in which inflammation and swelling of the orbital fat and muscles push the eye forward, causing proptosis (bulging), eyelid retraction, double vision, and in severe cases, compressive optic neuropathy that threatens sight. By selectively removing portions of the orbital walls, Dr. Vaishnav allows the expanded tissue to settle into the adjacent sinuses, reducing proptosis and relieving pressure on the optic nerve. Orbital decompression may also be performed following trauma or for other conditions that increase orbital volume or pressure. The procedure is tailored to each patient's anatomy and clinical needs to achieve the optimal balance of decompression, symmetry, and safety.
